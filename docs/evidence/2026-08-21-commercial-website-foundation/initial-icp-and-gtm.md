## Outcome

**Recommended initial ICP:** small specialist **CRO/performance marketing agencies** (about 5–30 people) serving multiple ecommerce/SaaS clients.

**Primary GTM channel:** founder-led, permissioned **design-partner outreach via CRO/agency communities and public teardown content**—not broad cold-email spam or paid search.

**Fallback ICP:** B2B SaaS product-marketing / competitive-intelligence leads, once PulseWatch supports Slack, evidence links, shared workspaces, and battlecard/export workflows.

## What I found

- PulseWatch’s public site is still a **coming-soon waitlist** with a Discord-based daily AI digest, so it should sell an honest **managed 30-day pilot**, not claim mature self-serve/enterprise capability.
- Agencies are the strongest first wedge because a single buyer can deploy many client/competitor watchlists and convert insights into recurring client recommendations and test backlogs.
- Public directional evidence from Visualping says marketing agencies are among its most active competitor-monitoring buyers and have the highest monitor counts per user. This is vendor-reported—not proof of PulseWatch WTP.
- Ecommerce pricing has demonstrated budget and frequent pain, but is a poor initial fit: specialists such as Prisync already provide SKU matching, price/stock history, and repricing.
- The core differentiator cannot be “AI change detection”: Visualping and Crayon already provide monitoring, summaries, importance scoring, and team integrations. PulseWatch should own the **agency action layer**: `signal → client impact → recommended test/action → client-ready brief`.
- All proposed price points, sales-cycle estimates, and validation gates are explicitly labelled as **unproven hypotheses**.
